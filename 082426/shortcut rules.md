But the key insight is that you can think of the BCC lattice as simply a stack of BCC bipyramids.

Then the shortcut axes, the 1.15... directions, have specific rules about how to activate within a single cell.

A BCC bipyramid or BCC cell has 1 apex-apex shortcut and 4 equatorial shortcuts.

Rule 1: For each shortcut in the lattice, there is an implied BCC cell where that shortcut is the apex-apex shortcut and it's surrounded by 4 equatorial shortcuts. A valid lattice has rules 2 and 3 followed for every implied cell.

Rule 2: if all 4 equator shortcuts are active, the apex-apex shortcut can't be active; its length must actually be sqrt(2), since the BCC cell is now an octahedron.

Rule 3: if the apex-apex shortcut is active, at most 2 equatorial shortcuts can be active (forming tetrahedra), and they cannot be neighbors joined at a 90-degree corner; they must be at the opposite side of the equator. Hence, a BCC cell can contain 1 or 2 tetrahedra at a time.
